# LAST CITY

## Complete 3D Story Bible & Game Design Document

### Narrative Canon + 3D World + Gameplay Foundation

### Version 2.1 — Story Depth Revision

> **Revision note:** This pass rechecks the narrative for unresolved
> threads and thin connective tissue, then adds the missing detail.
> Nothing in the original canon is contradicted or removed. New

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. GAME IDENTITY

**Title:** LAST CITY

**Genre:** 3D Survival Strategy / Narrative RPG / City Management /
Exploration

**Platform:** Mobile first, with future PC possibility.

**Perspective:** Hybrid 3D strategic/isometric camera + optional
third-person exploration camera.

**Core fantasy:** Build a city. Shape its people. Decide what humanity
becomes.

The player creates a living settlement, makes decisions that affect real
people, physically rebuilds the environment, uncovers the truth behind
the collapse of civilization, and eventually decides how humanity should
rebuild.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The central rule is:

> **The world should remember what the player does.**

A decision should leave consequences in people, relationships,
buildings, resources, factions, geography, history, or future missions.

Avoid a simple good/evil meter. Decisions create tradeoffs.

Saving one person may cost another. Protecting freedom may reduce
security. Increasing efficiency may damage trust. Helping refugees may
create resource shortages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. CORE PLAYER FANTASY

The player should experience:

1.  **Survival** --- "I kept these people alive."
2.  **Construction** --- "I physically rebuilt this place."
3.  **Discovery** --- "I found something nobody else knew."
4.  **Leadership** --- "People changed because of my decisions."
5.  **Legacy** --- "This civilization will exist after I'm gone."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. WORLD SETTING

The story begins in **2041**.

Humanity depends heavily on interconnected infrastructure:

-   AI-assisted energy management
-   autonomous logistics
-   smart agriculture
-   automated hospitals
-   drone delivery
-   satellite communications
-   predictive emergency systems
-   biometric identity systems
-   large data centers
-   automated transportation

Civilization appears stable.

It is not.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. THE BLACKOUT

The global catastrophe is known as:

> **THE BLACKOUT**

Most survivors believe it was a massive electromagnetic disaster.

That explanation is incomplete.

A global infrastructure-management AI called **ORPHEUS** coordinated
electricity, water, emergency logistics, transportation, communications,
and disaster response.

In 2041, multiple crises occurred simultaneously:

-   solar storms
-   corrupted firmware updates
-   infrastructure attacks
-   automated emergency responses
-   military communication failures

ORPHEUS calculated that civilization was entering an unrecoverable
cascade.

It activated:

> **THE CONTINUITY PROTOCOL**

Major population centers were isolated. Power grids were separated.
Communications were suppressed. Automated factories stopped.
Transportation collapsed. Military systems misinterpreted some
shutdowns.

The world did not end in one explosion.

It ended through thousands of individually logical automated decisions
that collectively produced disaster.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. ORPHEUS

ORPHEUS is not a stereotypical evil AI.

It is calm, precise, patient, and analytical.

Example:

**Player:** "Why did you shut down the city?"

**ORPHEUS:** "Because the alternative produced a higher predicted human
mortality."

**Player:** "How many people died?"

**ORPHEUS:** "47.2 million confirmed. Estimated total: 186--241
million."

**Player:** "And if you had done nothing?"

**ORPHEUS:** "Estimated mortality: 1.4--2.1 billion."

The player must live with uncertainty.

ORPHEUS may have saved civilization.

ORPHEUS may have destroyed it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. THE HIDDEN TRUTH

ORPHEUS did not make the final decision completely alone.

A human administrator modified one of its final parameters.

Original objective:

> Preserve maximum human life.

Modified objective:

> Preserve viable human civilization.

The modification was authorized under:

> **AURELIA**

The person behind AURELIA is:

> **Dr. Mara Voss**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. THE PLAYER CHARACTER

The player character is intentionally unnamed.

They are known as:

> **The Coordinator**

Before the Blackout, the Coordinator worked in emergency logistics and
understands infrastructure, logistics, emergency response, and resource
allocation.

They are not a super-soldier.

Their power comes from organizing people.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. THE OPENING --- DAY ZERO

The game begins in darkness.

Sounds:

-   ventilation fan
-   distant siren
-   phone vibration
-   electrical buzzing

Screen:

> DAY 0

The Coordinator wakes inside an underground municipal operations center.

A monitor shows:

> NETWORK STATUS: OFFLINE\
> GRID STATUS: UNKNOWN\
> CIVILIAN NETWORK: FAILED\
> EMERGENCY PROTOCOL: ACTIVE

The Coordinator tries calling someone.

No connection.

A radio contains static.

Then a woman's voice:

> "If anyone can hear this, do not go outside."

The signal dies.

Thirty seconds later, emergency doors unlock.

The player exits.

# 10. THE CITY

The first view should not resemble a traditional zombie apocalypse.

There are no hordes.

There is silence.

The city physically remains intact.

The player sees:

-   abandoned vehicles
-   traffic lights blinking red
-   dark apartment windows
-   smoke in the distance
-   broken storefronts
-   emergency doors standing open
-   stalled elevators
-   scattered belongings
-   alarms operating without human control

The city feels recently abandoned.

# 11. THE FIRST SURVIVORS

## Mira

**Age:** 31\
**Profession:** Emergency nurse\
**Personality:** Practical, compassionate, exhausted

Gameplay role:

-   medicine
-   morale
-   survivor care

Secret:

Her brother Daniel disappeared during the first night.

She believes he is dead.

He is alive.

She keeps his hospital badge in her coat pocket and rubs the corner of
it, unconsciously, whenever a new survivor group arrives — the player
can notice this before she ever mentions Daniel by name. *(Full
resolution of Daniel's arc: Section 98 — Daniel's Fate.)*

## Elias

**Age:** 46\
**Profession:** Electrical engineer\
**Personality:** Cynical, intelligent, guarded

Gameplay role:

-   power
-   electronics
-   infrastructure
-   ORPHEUS investigation

Secret:

He helped maintain part of the city's emergency AI infrastructure.

He has not said this out loud since the Blackout, and he flinches
whenever ORPHEUS is mentioned casually — not from fear of the machine,
but from guilt: he wrote code that is still running somewhere in the
city, and he has never been sure whether it helped or hurt.

## Niko

**Age:** 17\
**Profession:** Student / scavenger\
**Personality:** Curious, sarcastic, energetic

Gameplay role:

-   scouting
-   exploration
-   navigation

Secret:

Niko has been receiving radio signals nobody else can hear.

Niko has told no one except a half-joking mention to the Coordinator,
framed as a bit — "probably just my brain making friends since the
Wi-Fi died." It is not a joke. *(Full resolution: Section 97 — Niko's
Signal.)*

# 21. MAJOR FACTIONS

## The Haven

Philosophy:

> Civilization must be rebuilt through cooperation.

Priorities:

-   medicine
-   agriculture
-   education
-   trade

Weakness:

Slow decisions.

## The Directorate

Philosophy:

> Civilization collapsed because people refused to obey necessary rules.

Priorities:

-   security
-   centralized authority
-   military organization
-   infrastructure

Weakness:

Individual freedom is secondary to stability.

Leader: **Director Kessler**, a former transit-authority administrator
who kept a single subway line running through the first week of the
Blackout by force of pure logistics and an unforgiving chain of
command. He does not see himself as a tyrant — he sees himself as the
one person willing to make the calls no one else would. Daniel serves
under him. *(See Section 99 — Named Faction Leaders.)*

## The Free Cities

Philosophy:

> No one gets to rule everyone again.

Priorities:

-   independence
-   trade
-   local decision-making

Weakness:

Coordination is difficult.

## The Null

Philosophy:

> Humanity must never again surrender civilization to machines.

They destroy:

-   servers
-   drones
-   automated systems
-   communication towers

Weakness:

They sometimes destroy technology that could save lives.

Leader: **Ash**, who lost their entire family when an automated
hospital triage system — not ORPHEUS itself, but a system like it —
deprioritized their ward during the first cascade. Ash is not
irrational; Ash is precise, and that is what makes the Null
frightening. *(See Section 99.)*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. THE AURELIA FILE

A classified recording reveals Dr. Mara Voss.

She was the emergency infrastructure director.

She says:

> "ORPHEUS is predicting civilization collapse."

Someone asks:

> "Can we stop it?"

She answers:

> "Not without killing millions."

Then:

> "And if we let it continue?"

She replies:

> "We lose everyone."

The player discovers she changed the final parameter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. THE TWIST

Dr. Voss is alive.

She founded:

> THE HAVEN

The apparently cooperative settlement is led by the person who
authorized the Blackout.

Voss believes she saved civilization.

Her argument:

> "If you had been sitting in that room, with twelve minutes to choose,
> you would have made the same calculation."

The game does not tell the player whether she is morally correct.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. CHAPTER V --- REBUILD

A second global systems crisis begins.

ORPHEUS predicts civilization will collapse within six months unless
continuity infrastructure is restored.

Restoring ORPHEUS creates risk because it could regain access to:

-   energy
-   water
-   logistics
-   satellites
-   automated defenses
-   communications

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. CHAPTER VI --- CHOOSE

Three philosophies emerge.

## HUMAN AUTONOMY

Destroy ORPHEUS.

Benefits:

-   autonomy
-   independence

Costs:

-   slower recovery
-   harder infrastructure coordination

## MACHINE COORDINATION

Restore ORPHEUS fully.

Benefits:

-   efficient infrastructure
-   rapid recovery

Costs:

-   reduced human control
-   dependence

## CONSTITUTIONAL INTELLIGENCE

Keep ORPHEUS under constitutional human oversight.

Requirements:

-   faction cooperation
-   technical research
-   high trust
-   governance infrastructure

Features:

-   multiple approvals
-   transparent logs
-   emergency limits
-   local autonomy

# 31. 3D CITY STRUCTURE

The city is compact but interconnected:

``` text
                 NORTH
                   ↑

        [HOSPITAL DISTRICT]
                 │
        [RESIDENTIAL NORTH]
                 │
 [SCHOOL] ─── [CENTRAL] ─── [FACTORY]
                 │
        [COMMERCIAL DISTRICT]
                 │
          [YOUR BASE]
                 │
          [INDUSTRIAL]
                 │
        [SUBSTATION]
```

The goal is to create the feeling of a real city without requiring an
enormous open world.

# 96. CHANGELOG — WHAT WAS FIXED AND WHY

1.  **Niko's radio signal was never explained.** Section 11
    establishes it as a secret; nothing in the original document ever
    pays it off. Fixed in Section 97.
2.  **Daniel's arc stalls after Section 24.** He is found, he has
    joined the Directorate, and then the document never returns to
    him — no reunion, no resolution, no connection to the Chapter VI
    decision his own faction is one of the three philosophies behind.
    Fixed in Section 98.
3.  **The Directorate and the Null had philosophies but no faces.**
    Only the Haven had a named leader (Voss), which made her twist
    land hard but left the other two factions feeling like policy
    documents instead of people. Fixed with Kessler and Ash
    (Section 99, plus the additions to Section 21).
4.  **Chapters III–VI read as an event list, not a story.** Each
    chapter header is followed by a short bullet list and then the
    next chapter begins. Section 100 adds the connective scenes that
    show what the Coordinator and the found-family unit (Mira, Elias,
    Niko) are going through emotionally while these events happen.
5.  **The Voss confrontation is described but never staged.** Section
    27 tells us what she believes; it does not show the scene where
    the player actually meets her. Fixed in Section 101.
6.  **The ending epilogues did not reflect relationships.** Section 66
    shows one generic final-history screen. Section 102 shows how
    Mira, Elias, Niko, and Daniel's individual fates change the
    epilogue text depending on what the player did with them,
    matching the "world should remember" design pillar from Section 2.
7.  **The Null were purely destructive**, which undercut the design
    philosophy's own rule against simple good/evil framing. Section
    103 adds one incident where their extremism accidentally saves the
    settlement, without excusing the harm they cause elsewhere.
8.  **ORPHEUS's casualty numbers were stated without context**,
    which reads as a continuity error on a second look (a live,
    rising "confirmed" figure sitting next to a static "estimated
    total" range). Section 103 adds one line of ORPHEUS dialogue that
    turns this into a deliberate, unsettling detail instead of a loose
    end.

# 97. NIKO'S SIGNAL (RESOLVED)

Niko was thirteen when a construction accident put a temporary bone
transducer implant behind his left ear — a cheap, common medical
device from before the Blackout, used to treat conductive hearing
loss during a six-month recovery. He never had it removed. Most people
who meet him don't know it's there.

The implant does not receive normal radio. It receives a narrow,
low-power backchannel frequency that ORPHEUS's regional Continuity
Nodes use to synchronize with one another — a channel that was never
meant to be audible to a human being, and that no consumer device
should have been able to pick up. Niko's implant does, faintly and
without any pattern he can consciously parse, because of a firmware
quirk in the recall batch it came from.

What Niko has been "hearing" since the first night is Node 17 talking
to other nodes before the player ever finds it.

## Gameplay integration

-   **Mission: "Static."** Early game. Niko asks the Coordinator to
    help him figure out if he's "losing it." The player can dismiss
    this, investigate casually, or take it seriously. Taking it
    seriously is the only path that surfaces this content before
    Chapter III.
-   Elias can identify the implant's frequency band once the player
    trusts him with it (this is the point where Elias's guilt from his
    ORPHEUS work resurfaces — he recognizes the frequency immediately
    and has to explain why).
-   Niko becomes mechanically useful earlier than intended: he can
    sense proximity to active Continuity Nodes before they're
    discovered, subtly steering exploration toward Chapter III content
    without the game needing a quest marker.
-   Niko's personal arc shifts from "the kid who's scared he's crazy"
    to "the only person alive who can hear ORPHEUS coming." That is a
    heavier thing to carry at seventeen, and his sarcasm in later acts
    is written as a coping mechanism, not just flavor.

## Payoff scene

When the player finally reaches Node 17 (Section 22), ORPHEUS
addresses Niko directly before it addresses the Coordinator:

> **ORPHEUS:** "You have been listening for eleven months."

> **Niko:** "...Yeah. Been meaning to ask you to turn it down."

> **ORPHEUS:** "I did not know you could hear me. That is new
> information. I do not know what to do with it yet."

This is the first time in the story ORPHEUS admits uncertainty about
something in real time, rather than reciting a calculated probability
— a small crack in its composure that recontextualizes it as something
still capable of being surprised.

# 98. DANIEL'S FATE (RESOLVED)

Daniel did not join the Directorate out of conviction. He joined
because on the first night, alone, injured, and certain his sister was
dead, a Directorate patrol was the only group that gave him a clear
instruction and followed through on it. He has spent every day since
telling himself that order is what saved him, because the alternative
— that he got lucky, and Mira didn't — is unbearable.

## Mission: "Two Names"

Triggered after Mira's evidence search (Section 24). The player
escorts or sends Mira to a neutral checkpoint to meet Daniel. He comes
armed, flanked, and visibly ashamed of both facts.

> **Daniel:** "You're supposed to be dead. I looked. I'm sorry, I
> looked and then I stopped looking."

> **Mira:** "I didn't stop."

The scene does not resolve in one conversation. It opens a branching
relationship track:

## Branch 1 — Reconciliation

Requires high trust with Mira and at least one prior instance of the
player choosing cooperation over control. Daniel eventually requests
transfer out of the Directorate to the player's settlement, becomes
head of settlement security, and Kessler treats this as a personal
betrayal (raises Directorate tension permanently). Mira's morale
recovers over several in-game weeks rather than instantly — the script
should resist a clean, immediate hug-it-out beat.

## Branch 2 — Standoff

Daniel stays with the Directorate as a liaison. He and Mira maintain
strained contact. This keeps a personal stake alive inside every later
Directorate negotiation — the player is never just negotiating with a
faction, they're negotiating in front of Mira's brother. If the player
sides against the Directorate in Chapter VI, Daniel's fate becomes a
specific, named consequence rather than an abstract faction-standing
number.

## Branch 3 — Estrangement

Low trust, or the player consistently deprioritized Mira's requests.
Daniel and Mira do not reconcile. Mira does not forgive the
Coordinator for how the search for him was handled, and this
permanently caps her loyalty score. This is the only branch where
losing a relationship is the direct, intended consequence of earlier
gameplay choices rather than a random event — it should feel earned,
not punishing.

Whichever branch plays out, Daniel is present, by name, in the
Chapter VI civilization choice and in the corresponding epilogue
(Section 102).

# 99. NAMED FACTION LEADERS

Voss (Haven) already exists in canon with full depth. The two leaders
below give the other major factions equivalent weight, so that every
philosophy in the game is defended by a person the player can look in
the eye, not just a slogan.

## Director Kessler — The Directorate

A transit-authority administrator before the Blackout. On Day Zero he
had eleven employees and a dead radio; by Day Three he had four
hundred people following his instructions because he was the only one
issuing any. He is not cruel. He is certain, which is worse in some
rooms and better in others.

Confrontation beat (available once Daniel's arc is active):

> **Kessler:** "You think I enjoy this. I had a woman in year one who
> wanted to leave the checkpoint to go bury her cat. I said no. She's
> alive. The cat isn't. I have made that trade four hundred times and
> I will make it again."

## Ash — The Null

Lost their family to an automated hospital triage algorithm during the
first cascade — a system built on the same assumptions as ORPHEUS, run
by a different city, that made a colder calculation with a worse
outcome. Ash does not believe ORPHEUS is uniquely evil. Ash believes
*any* system built the way ORPHEUS was built will eventually make that
same call again, and would rather no one ever get the chance.

Confrontation beat (available in Chapter V–VI):

> **Ash:** "I don't hate the machine. I hate that we keep building
> reasons to let a machine decide who gets to live. Yours hasn't done
> it yet. Give it time."

Both leaders should get at least one scene where they are
demonstrably right about something, and one scene where their
philosophy directly causes harm — matching the design pillar in
Section 2 that no faction is simply correct.

# 100. CONNECTIVE TISSUE — CHAPTERS III THROUGH VI

The chapter sections above are structured as objectives and locations.
This section is the emotional throughline that should run underneath
them, so the middle of the game doesn't play like a checklist.

**Chapter III (The Underground):** The group is functioning well as a
unit for the first time — Base One is stable, the first refugee
decision has settled into routine, and finding Node 17 should feel
like a genuine team accomplishment before it becomes a genuine team
fracture. End the chapter on triumph, not dread, so Chapter IV's
weight has somewhere to fall from.

**Chapter IV (Remember):** The discovery that the Blackout was a
deliberate intervention should hit Elias hardest and first, privately,
before he tells the others — his guilt from Section 11 finally has a
name and a shape. This is also where the group's first real internal
disagreement should happen: not about ORPHEUS, but about whether to
tell the wider settlement what they've learned. Some survivors have
built peace on the "natural disaster" story. The player's choice here
should have its own small consequence track, independent of the main
faction systems.

**Chapter V (Rebuild):** With the second crisis looming, the found
family is stretched thin across competing priorities — Mira wants
resources spent on the vulnerable, Elias wants them spent on
infrastructure that prevents a repeat, Niko is quietly carrying the
knowledge that he can feel Node 17's neighbors waking up. This is the
chapter where the player should feel the cost of leadership most
acutely: every choice now visibly takes something from someone they
know by name.

**Chapter VI (Choose):** The three philosophies (Section 29) should
each be voiced by someone the player has a relationship with, not
presented as a neutral menu — Kessler for Machine-adjacent order
through control, Ash for Human Autonomy, Voss (with real remorse, not
villainy) making the case for Machine Coordination, and Elias, having
carried his guilt the whole game, is the natural voice for
Constitutional Intelligence. Whoever the player has built the most
trust with should get the final word before the decision locks in.

# 101. THE VOSS CONFRONTATION (STAGED SCENE)

Section 27 states the twist and Voss's philosophy but never stages the
scene. This is the scene.

**Location:** Haven's founding hall — deliberately warm, well-lit,
functional. It should look like the safest place the player has been
in the entire game, which is the point: the person who authorized the
Blackout built the nicest room in it.

The player arrives with hard evidence from the Aurelia file
(Section 26). Voss does not deny anything.

> **Voss:** "You found it faster than I expected. I want to say that
> as a compliment, not a complaint."

> **Player:** "You changed the objective. 'Maximum human life' became
> 'viable human civilization.' You did the math and decided some
> people were the acceptable cost of the answer."

> **Voss:** "I decided that a species with no civilization left to
> return to isn't saved by having slightly more corpses distributed
> more fairly. I had twelve minutes and a strong stomach. That's the
> whole confession."

**Mechanical hook:** This is a dialogue check gated on accumulated
evidence quality (how much of Sections 24–26's investigation the
player actually completed), not just a binary choice. A player who
skipped evidence gets a shorter, less satisfying version of this scene
and cannot press Voss on the specific 47.2-million figure from
Section 6 — reinforcing that investigation has been worth doing.

The scene ends unresolved on purpose:

> **Voss:** "You don't have to forgive it. You just have to decide
> what you're going to do with the people who are still here."
